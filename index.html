The Ecological Reality

Maine’s Frontline Tick Crisis

Maine represents ground zero for the expanding deer tick epidemic, where 1 in 3 dogs test positive for Anaplasmosis and 1 in 8 test positive for Lyme disease. Decades of living in this environment have made one reality undeniable: existing countermeasures fail to prevent tick entry and transmission.

01 / RETROACTIVE DEFENSE

Post-Attachment Latency

Oral isoxazolines and topicals require biological uptake through blood feeding. Pathogens like Borrelia burgdorferi (Lyme) and Anaplasma transfer during early salivary exchange, meaning chemical neutralization occurs after the host has already been exposed.

02 / VISUAL BLINDSPOT

Nymphal Invisibility

Manual tactile checks regularly miss nymphal-stage ticks, which are smaller than a sesame seed. In dense animal fur and undercoats, unengorged vectors remain entirely undetectable to handlers until feeding is well underway.

03 / DOMESTIC VULNERABILITY

Cross-Vectoring into Homes

Companion animals act as domestic vectors, carrying live, unattached ticks out of the woods and directly into family living spaces, beds, and vehicles—placing families at constant secondary risk.

While the Northeast represents ground zero, this vulnerability extends to a national market of over 65 million dog-owning households, demanding a scalable, pre-attachment detection solution.

Research Hypothesis & Protection

Pre-Attachment Bio-Acoustic Detection

Rather than waiting for chemical ingestion or relying on fallible visual inspections, our research tests the ability to capture distinct micro-mechanical signatures created by arachnid movement at the skin boundary.

The Sensing Architecture Concept

TJH Innovations is engineering a wearable bio-acoustic sensor architecture designed to detect the mechanical vibrations of crawling ticks before attachment and pathogen transmission can occur. By utilizing perimeter contact sensing and motion filtering to eliminate everyday pet activity artifacts, our approach aims to close the critical defense gap left by reactive treatments.

Tracy Hendricks

Tracy is a lifelong Maine resident, avid outdoor enthusiast, and the visionary founder of the TJH bio-acoustic detection system.
